# Skylabs --- Content and Brand Voice Guidelines

## Purpose

Skylabs content should build recognition and trust by demonstrating a
point of view about technology and real-world work, understanding of
industry workflows, product and engineering depth, thoughtful execution
and a credible company identity.

Do not post merely to appear active. Every post should offer a useful
idea, insight, example or reason to understand Skylabs.

## Voice

**Desired:** thoughtful, sharp, grounded, confident, specific and
human.\
**Avoid:** generic, breathless, self-important, jargon-heavy, salesy and
exaggerated.

Test: Would this sound credible coming from a small but serious product
company that is actually doing the work?

## Company content

Company content should sit between a dry corporate announcement and a
personal founder story.

Preferred structure: 1. Start with an observation, tension, question or
industry-specific problem. 2. Develop one clear idea. 3. Explain why it
matters. 4. Connect it to Skylabs only where relevant. 5. End with a
useful takeaway or restrained company statement.

Do not turn every post into a founder chronology. The origin story can
be told separately when useful.

## Hooks

Good hooks are specific, connected to the post and supportable.
Directions that resonated with Jeyasurya: - "Not every industry needs
another AI chatbot." - "The world runs on industries that software
wasn't always built for." - "We're building software for industries that
deserve better tools." - "A fleet generates data. Running a fleet
requires decisions." - "Jewellery design doesn't end when the image
looks good."

These are examples, not mandatory slogans. Avoid repeating one formula
until it becomes predictable.

## Writing principles

-   **Substance over announcement:** give readers a reason to care.
-   **Problems before products:** explain the real problem before
    introducing a solution when appropriate.
-   **Evidence over adjectives:** prefer workflows, examples, case
    studies, demos and sourced metrics.
-   **Specificity over generic AI talk:** explain what changes, for whom
    and under what conditions.
-   **Restraint over hype:** do not imply a product solves an entire
    industry's problems.
-   **One post, one main idea:** avoid cramming unrelated messages
    together.

## Language to avoid

Avoid unless there is a specific reason: - "We're
thrilled/excited/delighted to announce..." - "Revolutionising the
industry." - "Disrupting the space." - "Pushing the boundaries of
innovation." - "The future is here." - "Game-changing." - "Cutting-edge
solutions" without concrete explanation. - "Unlocking limitless
potential."

## Research and factual standards

For case studies and industry posts: - Prefer primary sources: company
engineering blogs, official reports, regulators, incident statements,
technical documentation and original case studies. - Attribute
vendor-published results to the vendor/case study; do not present them
as independently verified. - Verify dates, numbers, company names and
incident details. - Separate facts from analysis and opinion. - Never
invent statistics, customer quotes, savings or outcomes. - Use current
sources for current claims. Explain uncertainty or omit disputed claims.

## Visual direction

Use approved product screenshots, industry-relevant photography, simple
explanatory diagrams, editorial typography and restrained layouts. Avoid
generic AI stock imagery, robot heads, glowing brains and random
abstract technology graphics. Never use client assets or sensitive
operational data without permission.

## Platform adaptation

LinkedIn: strong opening, short readable paragraphs, one central
argument, concrete examples, credible sources and restrained CTA. Use
carousels when sequence or visual explanation adds value. Do not copy
LinkedIn posts verbatim to Instagram or X without adapting the format.
